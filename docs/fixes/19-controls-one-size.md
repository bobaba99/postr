# Fix 19 — a selected block's controls are one size on screen at every zoom

**Plan item:** 19 (from the owner's answer to fix 03's question about the 64 px gutter, 2026-09-30) · **Branch:** `fix/19-controls-one-size` · **Status:** steps 1 to 8 done (reproduced, the lead's defaults from the owner's MVP triage of 2026-10-06, fixed, measured in three engines, falsified); step 9's one round (the browser, through the user's entry points) done and its findings answered (section 9: F1 and F2 corrected; F3 first put to the Later list, then fixed by the lead's decision: zoomed out under 35 %, the threshold measured, a handle row wider than its block draws only its move button; a first version that did so at every zoom took Replace and Crop from small images and logos at the fits, and was corrected). Review budget: a simple feature (a display aid), one round; the F3 change has had no review round of its own (the lead's call).

## 1. Symptom

A researcher selects a block and zooms. The square handles, the round move and delete buttons, the type label, the rotate control under the block, a table's row and column strips, crop mode's edges and bar and a group's handles grow and shrink with the poster: 2 px handles at the 20 % floor (nothing to grab), 100 px at the 10× ceiling, 14 px at the 1280 × 800 fit (under the 24 px WCAG 2.5.8 minimum). At the large fits they leave the canvas or go under the zoom bar: an image at the sheet's left edge has its move button 112 px left of the canvas, over the sidebar (2560 × 1440); a logo at the bottom of a portrait poster has its rotate control under the zoom bar. Selecting a block at the fit can make the canvas scroll.

Reproduced on main `8722c30` by the reproducer, and re-measured here on main `e09c0ea` with the harness as committed (section 4).

## 6. Root cause

**A — the controls are drawn in the sheet's units, inside its zoom.** The sheet (`#poster-canvas`) is laid out in poster units (10 per inch) and zoomed by `transform: scale(zoom)` (`PosterEditor.tsx`). Every selection control is a descendant, and each was sized and placed in those units: handles 10 units with 5-unit squares (`resizeHandles.tsx`; its header said "px"), the round buttons 18 units, the row 24 units above the block, the stem 12 long, the rotate 30 below, a table's strips 8 thick 10 out and its grips 6 wide, crop edges 5 × 12 and its bar 18-unit buttons, a group's handles the same component. So on screen each was units × zoom (H1, MEASURED), while what they must fit against does not scale: the 64 px gutter, the canvas's edges, the zoom bar.

**B — the zoom bar sits inside the bottom gutter.** Its buttons span 17–42 px above the canvas's bottom, so a control under a block at the sheet's bottom edge can meet it at any size (the 4 R cases a plain counter-scale left, H3). Hence Q3.

**C — main's sizes are under 24 px even at 100 %** (handles 10, squares 5, strips 8, grips 6, crop edges 5; H2). One size at those values fails WCAG everywhere; the sizes had to change, not only stop scaling. Hence Q1.

**D — a block small on screen crowds one-size controls.** At main's scale, controls shrank with the block; at one size, a 6 × 4 px logo at the floor carries 24 px handles that overlap (the reproducer's mutant: logo 0 → 36 overlaps at the floor). Hence Q2. The title at the 1280 fit is 22.4 px tall inside its selection border (25.2 outside), so even the owner's rule needs the box the handles sit on, as rendered (the title stores 45 units and renders 18.1), and a rule for one axis under 24 px (section 7).

**E (found here) — lengths divided by the zoom are painted on the sheet's pixel grid.** Layout is exact to a fraction of a pixel, but a browser paints boxes, borders and clipped backgrounds snapped to whole pixels of the element's own space, and draws a filter's result at that space's resolution; inside the sheet a pixel of that space is `zoom` screen pixels. So a 20 px circle written as `calc(20px / zoom)` was drawn on a grid of 3.39 px at the 2560 fit and of 10 px at the ceiling: uneven rounds (Chromium), blurred then rounded-square buttons (WebKit, the drop shadow's filter and the clip), pills and a label set off its pill at 10× (H7, by eye on screenshots); and a border is rounded up to one whole pixel of that space, 10 px on screen at 10× (H8, MEASURED). This is why the drawing is now done in px inside boxes scaled back by the zoom (section 7).

**What this does not explain:** the group frame drawn from stored geometry (Gf; the stored-against-rendered family of records 02 and 03); the format toolbar's snapshot position (Tf); the layout overlaps of groups, crop mode and table strips (H4; Q5–Q7); the zoom bar's own small buttons (Q9); record 03's rotated block hidden by 21–62 px at the fit (the block, not its controls).

## 7. Fix

**One variable, one helper module** (`selectionLayout.ts`). The sheet sets `--postr-zoom` next to its `scale(zoom)` (`PosterEditor.tsx`). Every control is then placed and drawn so the zoom cancels:
- **Where a control sits** (its offset from the block's edge) is a length in the sheet divided by the zoom: `ctl(px)` = `calc(px / var(--postr-zoom, 1))`, as the lead's Option A says.
- **What a control draws** is laid out in plain px inside a box scaled back by the same factor, `UNZOOM` = `scale(calc(1 / var(--postr-zoom, 1)))` (`UNZOOM_X`/`UNZOOM_Y` for a strip whose length follows the block), about the corner or edge the box is placed by. This departs from the letter of the lead's decision ("every size … with one helper, calc(N px / zoom)"): sizes written that way measured one size (S 0 in three engines, the first version of this fix) but painted badly (cause E). In the scaled boxes the circles, squares, borders, shadows and the label's text are drawn on the screen's own grid, crisp in all three engines from the floor to the ceiling (section 8). The lead's constraint is kept: no transform goes on a button the stylesheet animates (`index.css`'s hover and press rules); the boxes that scale hold the buttons (the row, the rotate control's box), and the rotate button keeps only main's own counter-rotation (it carries `data-no-anim`). `calc()` with `var()` inside `scale()` was checked in Chromium, Firefox and WebKit (MEASURED: the computed matrices were 0.25 for a variable of 4 in each).

**Q1, sizes.** Every grabbable control has a 24 × 24 px hit area: resize handles (8 px square inside, a 1 px border), the round buttons (a 20 px circle: the colour painted inside 2 px of padding, `backgroundColor` so the shorthand does not reset the clip; a drop shadow on what is painted), the rotate control, a crop edge (a 16 × 6 px pill inside), the crop bar's buttons, a selected table's strips (24 px reaching outward, the 8 px band at the inner edge) and grips, a group's handles. The label: 20 px tall, 10 px type. The stem: 1.5 × 16 px. A group's outline: four 1.5 px strips with 4 px dashes every 7 px (scaled back across; a dashed border would grow with the zoom). Places: handles centred on their corners and edge midpoints (half a hit area out); the row's hit areas end 14 px above the block (12 px of the handles' reach and 2 px), the rotate control's start 14 px under it (`ROW_LIFT`, `ROTATE_GAP`); the crop bar's top 4 px under the image (as on main in units); a selected table's strips end 2 px outside the table (as on main in units).

**Q2, a block small on screen** (`blockControls`): from its padding box on screen (its rendered size less the selection border: the handles are centred on that box), measured by a ResizeObserver (`selectionRoom.ts`; a block growing with its text stores another height), times the zoom. No n or s handle under 72 px wide, no e or w under 72 px tall; under 24 px on both axes only the bottom-right corner, the move button and nothing else (no label, delete, replace, crop or rotate); the label from 120 px wide. **Not in the owner's rule, added here:** one axis under 24 px drops the near row (or column) of handles, so the bottom (or right) one stays without overlapping it; the title at 100 % (18 px tall inside its border) would otherwise draw two rows of 24 px hit areas 18 px apart. The same rule draws a group's handles, from its frame's box. The owner may want another rule for thin blocks.

**Q3, the rotate control** (`rotateFitsBelow`, `useSelectionRoom`): where it would sit below the block (turned with the block's rotation) is tested against the visible canvas (within half a pixel) and the zoom bar (`[data-postr-canvas-chrome]`, beside the scroll container). When below has no room it becomes the last button of the handle row, without its stem; "above the block" is read as "in the row above it" rather than a second floating control above the row. Read on each render of a selected block (the editor renders every block when the canvas scrolls) and when the canvas changes size without a render (a ResizeObserver on the canvas). Dragging it still turns the block about its centre (`useBlockDrag` reads the block's centre and the pointer, not the control).

**Q4** was not needed: R found no handle row off the canvas after the counter-scale (0 of 66, three engines), so no clamp was built.

**Table strips and grips.** One size while the table is selected; an unselected table's strips and grips stay as on main, in sheet units. At one size an unselected table would carry an invisible 26 px band left of it and above it that takes clicks meant for its neighbours (at the floor 130 units, 13 in). Whether strips show on an unselected table at all is Q7 (Later).

**Crop mode.** Its edges and bar one size; the frame line around the kept area stays in sheet units (2 units), like the block's own selection border.

**After review round 1** (section 9). On a turned block the handle row's counter-rotation turns about the row's own centre: `translateX(-50%) translateY(ctl(-12)) rotate(-θ) translateY(ctl(12)) UNZOOM`, still scaled back about its bottom edge, so its bottom stays 14 px above the block and its centre 26 px out from the block's top edge at every turn; turned about the bottom edge, as first written, it hung back over the block's own handles near 180° (F1). A crop edge handle carries `data-no-anim`: it is a `role="button"` div, and index.css's transition on `[role='button']` animated its scale back for about 120 ms after each zoom change (F2).

**After review round 1: F3, a handle row wider than its block, zoomed far out** (the lead's decision, 2026-10-06: a fix must not ship a way to delete a block with a click meant for another block; corrected the same day: not at the cost of Replace and Crop at editing zooms). `blockControls` takes the sheet's zoom and the row's parts that vary (`row`: the type label's width on screen, and whether the block has replace and crop) and compares the width of the row's buttons (`handleRowWidth`: the move button, delete, and an image's or logo's replace and crop, 24 px each and 4 px apart: 52 px, or 108 px for an image or a logo) with the block's width on screen (Q2's width: the rendered padding box from the ResizeObserver, times the zoom). Where they are wider **and** the zoom is under `OVERVIEW_ZOOM` (0.35, measured: section 9), the row draws only the move button (and the label by its own rule, which never meets this case: no row of buttons is 120 px wide); no delete, replace or crop button and no rotate control, below the block or in the row. From 0.35 up the row is whole however narrow the block: a 3 in image or logo keeps Replace, Crop, Delete and the rotate control at the fits and at 100 % (claim Fr). The first version cut such a row at every zoom, which took Replace and Crop from a 3 in image or logo at the 1280 fit, at 100 % and at the 2560 fit; the reviewer's neighbour coverage at the fit was no regression (0.37 % against main's 0.35 %, the reviewer's figure, UNVERIFIED here), and crop has no other way in. The rotate control is not counted in the width: it joins the row only as Q3's fallback, as its last button, and then every delete, replace and crop button lies at least 14 px inside the block's left and right edges where the buttons fit (INSPECTED, from the row's geometry: the row grows by 28 px about its centre). **Not in the lead's rule, added here and accepted by the lead:** where the buttons are drawn, the label shows only if the row has room for it too, so a drawn row is never wider than it must be. In the first version, counting the label against the buttons would have taken them away again on a 3 in image zooming in between 429 % (the label starts) and 586 % (the row with it fits); with the cut only under 0.35, where no block wide enough for its label has buttons wider than itself, that cannot happen. The label's width is its computed width, read on each render of a selected block that draws it (`useSelectionRoom`: its layout width is its width on screen, the row being drawn in px and scaled back, and the selection's pop does not scale it), kept while the label is not drawn (reading 0 then would show it, and the next render hide it again: a loop, INSPECTED), 0 before it is first drawn and in jsdom. The row stays sized to its buttons (`width: max-content`, as before) and no `pointer-events: none` was added (the row's gaps take a click and stop it, as before). A group's frame draws handles only and passes no `row`.

**Where delete, replace and crop are, zoomed out under 35 %** (INSPECTED; the keys and the menu TESTED, section 8): Delete and Backspace delete every selected block (`PosterEditor.tsx`'s window key handler, not while a text field has focus); the right-click menu's Delete serves every block but the table and the text-like ones (title, authors, heading and text keep the browser's menu; the table's own menu acts on its cells). Replace and Crop come back on zooming in to 35 % (or until the image is wider than its row).

**Kept as they were:** the controls stay inside their block (a rotation carries them); every pointer handler (they already divide by the zoom); the thumbnail's and comment mode's markers (`data-postr-resize-handle` now names a direction; the attribute is still there); `useBlockDrag`. The resize handles' header and `fit-check.mjs`'s Hh header now say what is true.
