## 1. Symptom

A researcher nudges a block, or types a last word, or presses A+, and closes the tab, reloads, follows "Back to My Posters", presses the browser's Back or types another address. The browser asks "Leave site? Changes you made may not be saved", she answers Leave, and when she comes back the change is gone. The save waits 800 ms after an edit; the leave warning (fix 27) appears, but the save it fires almost never reaches the server in Chromium and WebKit (Chromium 1 of 40 cells over two runs, a tab close; WebKit 0 of 40), and in Firefox only sometimes. In Firefox, leaving while a slow save is out can even put the older poster over the newer one. Back inside the app to the dashboard keeps the change, but closing the tab right after that loses it with no warning at all.

Review round 1 found the dashboard's other half (R1-1): a researcher whose saves are failing ("Not saved — retrying…") presses Back to the dashboard: the change is never saved, nothing says so, and the poster opened again from its card shows the older copy; or a slow save is out, she types again, goes to the dashboard and closes the tab: lost with no warning. And with a slow next page (the old page stays up until the new one answers) a newer save could land before an older one, which then overwrote it (R1-2).

**What it does not cover.**
- A request the page sends at `pagehide` as the tab closes is not seen by Playwright's routes in any engine (control LK, every run: the page's target has gone), so a tab close is measured through what the page sends at `beforeunload`. A keepalive request made a moment (1–3 ms) before a tab closes can be missed the same way (Firefox, L3c: the page's record shows it made, the routes never saw it); L3c counts such a request as sent.
- The keepalive flag itself does not change what the instrument sees: with the flag taken out (mutant `L1-request-not-keepalive`), 12 of 12 Chromium cells (type and nudge, six ways) were still stored. A request made inside the leave's event reaches Playwright's routes before the page goes whether or not it is keepalive, and the fake answers within milliseconds. What the flag is for, a request still on the real network when the page goes, is the Fetch standard's and MDN's ("the browser will not abort the associated request if the page that initiated it is unloaded before the request is complete", MDN, `RequestInit`), not measured here: UNVERIFIED in a real browser over a real network.
- Playwright's routes answer the CORS preflight of the cross-origin PATCH. Review round 1's reviewer drove it without routes (R1-6, two real HTTP servers on loopback, the PATCH with the fix's headers, the preflight's answer delayed 0, 300 or 1,500 ms), and so did I with a copy of its probe (`<scratch>/c1/probe-cors-keepalive.mjs`, ports 5806–5807; not committed: it found no defect): the keepalive PATCH, sent at `beforeunload` or `pagehide`, then the tab closed or another address opened, arrived in 9 of 9 cells in Chromium and in Firefox, its OPTIONS first, and in WebKit in 6 of 6 when the tab closed with the request sent at `beforeunload` or another address opened (0 of 3 sent at `pagehide` as the tab closed: Playwright's WebKit fires only `beforeunload` on `page.close`, events `["beforeunload"]` against all four on a navigation, MEASURED with a copy of the reviewer's second probe, `<scratch>/c1/probe-webkit-pagehide.mjs`, port 5808); the same request without keepalive arrived in 2 of 9 in Chromium and in Firefox (only with no delay on the preflight; 0 of 6 delayed) and 0 of 9 in WebKit (MEASURED on loopback). ## 6. Root cause

**A.** The save made as the page is left cannot leave in time. Every save goes through `upsertPoster` (`src/data/posters.ts`), the Supabase client's `.update().eq().select().single()`, whose `fetch` is made only after `fetchWithAuth` (supabase-js 2.103.0, `dist/index.mjs`) awaits `getAccessToken()` → `auth.getSession()`, which takes the auth lock (`navigator.locks`) and reads storage: tasks, not microtasks. A `beforeunload` handler cannot wait (`useAutosave.ts`, main: `void flush()`), and once the dialog is answered the page goes: in Chromium the request was never made (16 of 20) or made too late (4); in WebKit made 3–38 ms after the leave began and cancelled as an ordinary request; Firefox keeps the old page running longer, so the outcome there is timing. fix 27's own test of this path (`keepWorkSafe.test.tsx`, "closing the tab sends the change at once") mocks `upsertPoster`, so the asynchronous read inside it was invisible to the suite: the instrument that measured it was built from the hook's side of the call.

**B.** One write at a time counts a write done when the client sees it end. A write the browser cuts off as the page goes ends on the client with a failure while the server may already hold it; `flush`'s queued write (main: `while (inFlightRef.current) await …` then write) then goes at once, and lands before the held one (Firefox, H4). This is fix 27's S8 (an older write landing after a newer one) reached through the cancellation, not through two writes in flight from the client's view.

**C.** The editor's unmount save (a route change inside the app) is the same ordinary request after the same asynchronous read (cause A), and the editor's leave dialog goes with the editor, so a tab closed right after loses the change silently (H5).

**Shared root cause and siblings** (looked for before the change): A is one mechanism behind every unloading way (five) and the unmount (C): the change waiting lives only in the hook, and the only way out is the client's asynchronous update. The sibling with the same mechanism outside the poster's data: `lib/diagnostics.ts` sends its batch at `pagehide` with `keepalive`, but only after `await supabase.auth.getSession()` (its own comment: "keepalive on the request only helps if the request is actually made"): MEASURED not sent (`LD-diagnostics-at-leave`, Chromium and Firefox: 0 batches when the page goes 1.5 s after a failure; the control, the page kept 6 s: 1 batch at 4.3 s). Not fixed (diagnostics, not the user's work; PLAN's Later list). Other writes at a leave, read for the same pattern (INSPECTED): the forced thumbnail capture at unmount (best effort by design, `useAutosave.ts` comment), the sidebar's drafts (`useSessionDraft`, `sessionStorage`: synchronous), the figure script (`localStorage`: synchronous); no version, export or name write is made at a leave.

**Review round 1's causes** (INSPECTED in `b44733d`, each confirmed by its measurement in section 4):

**D (R1-1).** The change waiting lived only in the editor's hook. Leaving the editor inside the app unmounts it: the unmount sent one write, queued behind any write out, with `aliveRef` already false, so a failure was never retried (`schedule` returns once the editor has gone), the leave listeners were removed with the editor, and nothing outside the hook knew of the change: the poster opened again loaded the server's older copy. The first version's comment "its page, if it closed, warned first" holds for a tab close, not for a route change. This is the cause fix 27 named twice (its R2-A3, the session ending, and its section 10's crash screen "Try again": "the change waiting to be saved lives only inside the hook"); its third face is fix 27's poster switch in a mounted editor (OF-03/OF-04), which drops the outgoing change. **F (R1-3).** Keepalive was the caller's choice: the leave's `flush` asked for it, but two callers waiting on one write resume in the order they began to wait, and the change's own save (its debounce, waiting first) wrote the change as an ordinary request while the page was going.

**How.** The hook keeps the session's access token as the client reports it (`onAuthStateChange`: the session on subscribing, then each refresh or sign-out), so the leave's write needs no asynchronous read. `upsertPoster(id, update, { keepalive, accessToken })` builds the request itself and calls `fetch` before it first waits. The hook's `leaveWrite` runs `flush` with keepalive; `flush` is unchanged otherwise (one write at a time: the leave's write is the one in flight, so an edit made after a Stay waits for it, L4s). The cut-off (since correction 1): `write` records the request it makes (`outRef`: keepalive or not); `beforeunload` and `pagehide` mark an ordinary one out as out at a leave (`markOutAtLeave`); if it then fails, `markCutOff` sets `cutOffRef` and a 10 s timer that clears it and writes the change waiting; `flush` returns false without writing while it is set, whoever calls it. Keepalive (since correction 1): `flush(…, { keepalive })` sets `keepaliveAskedRef`, which the next `write` takes and spends. The kept change (since correction 1): the hook's unmount, with a change pending and a session that can store it, records it (`hooks/keptChanges.ts` `recordKept`: the poster's doc and name, and a `handOver`) and leaves its retries, its leave listeners and its session subscription running (`attach`, `detachRef`); the first write of it that is stored ends it (`stop`), as does a change of signed-in user. `pages/Editor.tsx` opens a poster from `keptChange(id)` when there is one; the editor that mounts on it (or switches to it) calls `takeOver`, which stops the closed editor's saving, waits for its write out, and writes the poster as opened.

**How, since correction 2.** `write`, when an ordinary write fails with the page not yet being left, calls `noteFailure`: it opens a window (`failWindowRef`: a promise and a 200 ms timer that ends it); `markOutAtLeave` (at `beforeunload` and `pagehide`), finding the window open with a change pending, marks the cut-off as for a write out (`markCutOff`) and ends the window. `flush` records whether it found a write out when it was called (`waited`), and after waiting for it, while the window is open, waits for the window too, then for any write sent meanwhile. `flush`, finding after its wait that it may write nothing (cut off, or nothing pending), clears `keepaliveAskedRef` before it returns. `pages/Editor.tsx` reads `keptChange(posterId)` before `await loadPoster` and opens from it when the row is that poster and nothing is kept for it any more. `keptChanges.ts` `dropKept(id)` runs the keeper's `handOver` (its saving stopped, its change and listeners dropped), and `deletePoster` calls it after the delete succeeded, beside `forgetPosterScript` (record 07's precedent: a deleted poster's plot script goes the same way).

**How, since correction 3.** `data/posters.ts` throws `SaveError` on both save paths, carrying the HTTP status of the answer (the client's update: the PostgREST answer's `status`, which the client sets to 0 when its `fetch` failed; the keepalive answer: `res.status`); a `fetch` that rejects has no status. `write`'s catch reads the status first (as the diagnostic did, clamped) and runs `markCutOff` or `noteFailure` only when it is 0. `schedule`'s timer calls `flush(undefined, { fromTimer: true })`, and `flush` holds while the window is open a save that waits on a write out or that its timer started (`mayHold`), where it held only the first.

**Why this shape.** There is no version check on a poster's save to keep: every write is unconditional and the row's `updated_at` is set by the database's trigger (`posters_touch_updated_at`, `20260408000100_posters.sql`), so a write cannot be made conditional on another still out without a new column or function (conflict resolution: fix 27's R2-A2, the owner's call). The guard fix 27's saves use is the client's single writer; this keeps it and closes the one hole the leave opens (B). Sending the write at `beforeunload` rather than only at `pagehide` is what reaches the server when the tab is closed in every engine's instrument (a `pagehide` request at a close is not seen: section 3), and the write is a save of the change, so a user who answers Stay has it saved sooner, nothing worse. Not every save is keepalive: the client's own path refreshes an expiring token before it sends, which the hand-built request does not.

**Why correction 1's shape.** *The kept change* (R1-1): the reviewer offered a module-level save queue or a router blocker. A blocker is not available: the app's router is React Router's declarative `<BrowserRouter>` (`App.tsx`), and `useBlocker` is "Not available with Declarative" (its documentation, read 2026-10-17); a blocker would also need a new dialog of the app's own. A new queue would be a second copy of fix 27's retry and single-writer rules. So the hook that made the change keeps saving it with the same code after the editor has gone, and hands it to the editor opened again: one owner per poster, the rules unchanged. The change lives in the page's memory: a tab closed while it is kept gets the dialog and the leave's write, as in the editor. *The 10 s wait* (R1-2): with no version on a write, the client cannot know when a write the browser cut off has landed or never will; the instrument's held writes landed 2.5–5 s later, and a newer write sent within that time was overwritten (section 4). 10 s is past Supabase's documented default statement timeout for the `authenticated` role, which guests' sessions use too (8 s, "Timeouts", read 2026-10-17; this project's own setting not read), so a cut write that reached the database is applied or cancelled by then; one held before it reaches the database is not bounded by it. It bounds the window, it does not order the writes, and it costs a newer change made while a cut write is out whenever the page goes within those 10 s (section 10). Ordering on the server stays the owner's call (fix 27's R2-A2). *Keepalive at send time* (R1-3) and *the cut-off on the write out* (R1-4): the leave's state belongs to the request the page has out, whoever made it, not to the caller or the clock.

**Why correction 2's shape.** *The 200 ms window* (R2-1): the page cannot tell an engine's cut from a network failure (WebKit words both "Load failed"), and a leave event is the only sign the page is going; the gaps measured between WebKit's failure and its `beforeunload` were 2–12 ms (sections 4 and 8), so 200 ms leaves room for a slower device without reaching fix 27's next retry (2 s). A bounded choice, beside the 10 s settle, and the owner's to change: a real network failure in the 200 ms before a leave counts as the leave's cut too (section 10). Holding only the save that waited on the failed write (H10b's), not every save after a failure: a save started after the failure (back online, ⌘S, the editor's unmount, a take-over) is not the failure's, and holding it too cost "at once" in ten tests of fix 27's and this record's (TESTED: ten tests red when every save was held, three of them older than this fix: back online, `flushNow`'s answer after a failure, the sidebar's Duplicate; the other seven this record's, the kept change's); a save that starts by coincidence in the few ms between an engine's cut and its leave event is not held (section 10). *Spending the ask* (R2-3) on both of `flush`'s returns that write nothing, not only the one the reviewer's candidate took: the newest written 10 s after a cut-off is not a write at the leave either, and a page still being left then asks again at its next leave event. *Reading the kept change first* (R2-2): within one tab the kept change is the newest copy of its poster (the editor closed with it; nothing edits the poster meanwhile), so it is at least as new as anything the load can answer; keeping the entry until a loader read it was the alternative, a second lifetime for one change. *Dropping it at the Delete* (R2-4), where the user deletes, once the delete has succeeded (a delete that fails keeps the change: the card comes back, and so must its work); not at a 406 answer, which reads the state from an error and would also act on a delete made elsewhere (two tabs or devices: out of scope).

**Why correction 3's shape.** *Only a failure with no answer is a possible cut* (R3-1): the cut rules exist because the browser cancels a request the server may already hold, and a cancelled request has no answer; a server's error answer is the server saying it stored nothing (PostgREST answers an error after rolling its transaction back: from its documentation as I know it, not re-read for this record, UNVERIFIED; a gateway's 502 or 504 is the exception, section 6), so no older write can land later. The reviewer's alternative, keeping the rules and rewording the sentences, would have kept a 10 s delay for every user who stays after a server error near a leave, and a loss after the dialog for one who leaves; the status was already wanted by the diagnostic. A network failure stays counted (the page cannot tell it from an engine's cut: both are `TypeError` with no status). *Holding the timer's save* (R3-2), not every save: the ten tests correction 2 turned red when every save was held are the explicit saves (back online, ⌘S, the sidebar's Duplicate, the unmount, a take-over), which a user or the page asks for after the failure; a timer's save is nobody's request, and delaying it 200 ms at most after a failure is invisible (it was going to run then anyway, and the pill says "Not saved — retrying…" meanwhile). The alternative, keeping it as the owner's residual, leaves a gate the instrument can now make fail in the three engines.
