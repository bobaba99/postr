## 1. Symptom

Two kinds, found by the claims audit of 2026-10-05/06 and the owner's answers to it.

**What the pages say.** The Privacy Policy did not publish what Quebec's *Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector* (Law 25) asks a business to publish: the person in charge had no title ("reachable at the same address"), there were no governance policies (roles, retention and destruction, complaints), the rights had no way to use them beyond "email us", de-indexation, a 30-day reply, the absence of technology that identifies, locates or profiles, and privacy by default were missing, and it promised to "name a dedicated Data Protection Officer if and when legal thresholds require it". It named a hosting region (Oregon) and Standard Contractual Clauses nobody had confirmed, and said Resila was "registered" in Quebec where the owner confirmed "incorporated". The Cookies Policy said Postr does not read Global Privacy Control and keeps counting page views when a browser sends it. The Terms offered LaTeX export (now hidden), gave prices without saying tax is added, said nothing of an unused term ending at once on its refund, and sent every dispute "exclusively" to the courts of Montréal.

**What the product does.** A visitor whose browser sends Global Privacy Control (GPC) still loads Vercel Web Analytics; the feedback form ticks "Include console log" by default; deleting an account leaves four kinds of Postr entries in the browser, one of them carrying the account id; the analytics address of `/gallery/<id>` is not redacted; the counting request's Referer header carries the editor's address, `/p/<poster id>`; and where a new account accepts the Terms, only the English Terms are linked.

## 6. Root cause

- **GPC (H1, confirmed).** `App.tsx` rendered `<Analytics>` on every page with no condition, and no file read `navigator.globalPrivacyControl` (the Cookies Policy said so, and `cookiesStorageInventory.test.ts` pinned that the page must not claim otherwise). The vendor script does not read GPC either (its v0.1.3 source, read on production).
- **The poster id in the Referer (H2, confirmed; the alternative refuted).** The production script sends `fetch('/_vercel/insights/view', { method: 'POST', keepalive: true, … })`; under `Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin` a same-origin request carries the full page address. `beforeSend` cannot touch request headers.
- **`/gallery/:id` (H3, confirmed in the function).** `IDENTIFIER_ROUTES` was written before the gallery route; nothing tied the list to the router. Whether a beacon is ever sent for `/gallery/<id>` before its redirect lands is INSPECTED only (the script loads deferred, after the redirect's effect), so this is defence in depth.
- **The console log (H4, confirmed).** `useState(true)` and `setIncludeLog(true)` on open; the open effect decides what the user sees, the initial state does not (equivalent mutant, section 8).
- **Account deletion (H5, confirmed).** `LOCAL_KEYS` was a hand-kept list; four writers came later. One key holds the account id (`postr.welcome-seeded:<id>`), so clearing every marker by prefix would also clear another account's marker in the same browser and seed that account's welcome poster again.
- **The legal copy (H6, confirmed).** The pages were written as GDPR-first templates and patched per claim; nothing checked them against a list of what Law 25 and the owner require.

**What this does not explain.** Whether Vercel keeps the Referer header or the beacon's IP address, or derives a country from it (Vercel's side; UNVERIFIED). The audit's measurement of the development script's referrer (not re-run: `script.debug.js` is on a host this session may not load). Any legal sufficiency question.

**Checked and not shared:** the storage-inventory test (record 07) pins which keys the policy lists, not which ones deletion clears; the two lists drifted independently. Fix 23's sharing switch is why `/s/:slug` and `/gallery/:id` redirect; it did not cause H3.
