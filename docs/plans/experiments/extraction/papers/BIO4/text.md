# BIO4 — Does intermittent fasting extend lifespan in model organisms?

_Systematic review synthesis generated with Elicit (full-text extraction over 8 papers). This is the "paper" the extraction experiment reads; the per-paper findings below each carry the verbatim supporting quote Elicit extracted._

## Abstract

Intermittent fasting extends lifespan across evolutionarily divergent model organisms, with effects ranging from 20-40% in invertebrates to up to 30% in rodents. A controlled study in C. elegans demonstrated a 28% mean lifespan extension, while IF also effectively extends lifespan in yeast, flies, and worms. All eight reviewed studies supported the longevity-promoting effects of IF, with the consistency of findings across diverse species indicating activation of conserved longevity pathways. The mechanisms involve multiple interconnected pathways, including RHEB-1 and insulin/IGF-like signaling in C. elegans, the TOR pathway and circadian clock, and increased autophagy and mitochondrial respiratory efficiency. Notably, IF extends lifespan independent of total food intake and weight loss in rodents, suggesting that the temporal pattern of feeding rather than caloric reduction per se drives longevity benefits.

All included studies supported the hypothesis that intermittent fasting extends lifespan in model organisms[Sakiko Honjoh et al., 2009; D. Hwangbo et al., 2020; S. Anton & C. Leeuwenburgh, 2013; Lei Hou et al., 2016; T. Ching & Ao-Lin Hsu, 2025; L. Fontana, 2017; Su-Ju Lin et al., 2002; L. Fontana & L. Partridge, 2015]. The magnitude of lifespan extension varied by organism and IF protocol, with quantitative estimates ranging from 20-40% in invertebrates[T. Ching & Ao-Lin Hsu, 2025] to up to 30% in rodents[L. Fontana & L. Partridge, 2015]. The most precise measurement came from a controlled study in C. elegans, which reported a 28% mean lifespan extension[Lei Hou et al., 2016].

The molecular mechanisms underlying IF-induced longevity appear to involve multiple interconnected pathways. In C. elegans, the small GTPase RHEB-1 plays a crucial role, mediating IF effects through the insulin/IGF-like signaling pathway[Sakiko Honjoh et al., 2009]. Broader mechanistic themes include modulation of the TOR pathway and circadian clock[D. Hwangbo et al., 2020], increased autophagy and mitochondrial respiratory efficiency[S. Anton & C. Leeuwenburgh, 2013], and metabolic shifts involving regulation of metabolism and cell cycle/DNA damage responses[Lei Hou et al., 2016]. Notably, IF extends lifespan independent of total food intake and weight loss in rodents[L. Fontana & L. Partridge, 2015], suggesting that the timing of feeding rather than caloric reduction per se drives longevity benefits.

The consistency of findings across evolutionarily divergent organisms—from yeast[Su-Ju Lin et al., 2002] and worms[Sakiko Honjoh et al., 2009; Lei Hou et al., 2016; T. Ching & Ao-Lin Hsu, 2025] to flies[T. Ching & Ao-Lin Hsu, 2025] and rodents[L. Fontana & L. Partridge, 2015]—indicates that IF activates conserved longevity pathways. Reviews emphasize that IF can trigger similar biological pathways as continuous caloric restriction[S. Anton & C. Leeuwenburgh, 2013], though with potentially distinct mechanisms related to the temporal pattern of nutrient availability rather than overall caloric reduction. The observation that CR animals may experience intermittent fasting due to consuming food within 2-3 hours followed by 21-22 hours of fasting raises the possibility that some CR effects historically attributed to reduced calories may actually reflect fasting-refeeding cycles[L. Fontana, 2017].

## Per-paper findings

Each item is one paper's single most important finding as Elicit's full-text extraction reported it, followed by the verbatim supporting quote and the classified effect direction.

1. **T. Ching et al., 2025** — Intermittent fasting extends lifespan in model organisms such as worms and flies by 20-40% and enhances longevity in mice and rats, although conclusive evidence for human lifespan extension is absent. Supporting quote: "In organisms such as worms and flies, intermittent fasting has been shown to extend lifespan by 20-40%." (Effect direction: supports the hypothesis.)

2. **S. Anton et al., 2013** — Intermittent fasting regimens can trigger similar biological pathways as caloric restriction, which may lead to beneficial effects on longevity, as suggested by a growing body of literature. Supporting quote: "A growing body of evidence indicates that fasting periods and intermittent fasting regimens in particular can trigger similar biological pathways as caloric restriction. Evidence that this approach may have beneficial effects on longevity first appeared several decade ago (Carlson and Hoelzel, 1946). Since this time, a growing body of literature suggests that fasting periods and intermittent fasting regimens can trigger similar biological pathways as caloric restriction (i.e., increased autophagy and mitochondrial respiratory efficiency), which can result in a host of beneficial biological effects including increased circulation and cardiovascular disease protection, and modulation of reactive oxygen species and inflammatory cytokines (Lee and Longo, 2011)." (Effect direction: supports the hypothesis.)

3. **L. Fontana et al., 2017** — Calorie restriction without malnutrition is a robust intervention for extending healthspan and lifespan in multiple model organisms, with some caveats, and its effects may be partly due to intermittent fasting or time-restricted feeding. Supporting quote: "Thus, it is possible that the effects on health and longevity that we have noticed so far in the CR animals may be due, at least in part, to intermittent fasting or daily time-restricted feeding." (Effect direction: supports the hypothesis.)

4. **Su-Ju Lin et al., 2002** — Calorie restriction extends lifespan in Saccharomyces cerevisiae by increasing respiration through the shunting of carbon metabolism toward the mitochondrial tricarboxylic acid cycle. Supporting quote: "Calorie restriction (CR) extends lifespan in a wide spectrum of organisms and is the only regimen known to lengthen the lifespan of mammals." (Effect direction: supports the hypothesis.)

5. **D. Hwangbo et al., 2020** — Intermittent fasting extends lifespan in both vertebrate and invertebrate model organisms, sharing common mechanisms with caloric restriction such as the TOR pathway and circadian clock. Supporting quote: "Although the effects of IF on health and longevity have not been elucidated as clearly as those of CR, there is evidence indicating a positive effect of IF on aging [31,32]." (Effect direction: supports the hypothesis.)

6. **Lei Hou et al., 2016** — Intermittent fasting extends the mean lifespan of worms by 28%. Supporting quote: "CR and IF extend the mean lifespan of worms by 40% and 28%, respectively." (Effect direction: supports the hypothesis.)

7. **L. Fontana et al., 2015** — Intermittent fasting extends lifespan in rodents by up to 30%, independent of total food intake and weight loss. Supporting quote: "In rodents, both fasting for 24 hr every other day or twice weekly extends lifespan up to 30%, independent of both total food intake and weight loss." (Effect direction: supports the hypothesis.)

8. **Sakiko Honjoh et al., 2009** — Intermittent fasting extends the lifespan of Caenorhabditis elegans through a mechanism involving RHEB-1. Supporting quote: "Here we establish an IF regimen that effectively extends the lifespan of Caenorhabditis elegans" (Effect direction: supports the hypothesis.)
