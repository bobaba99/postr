# BIO2 — Do high-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants reduce genome-wide off-target editing?

**Review summary (Elicit):** High-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants reduce genome-wide off-target editing by 10- to 1,500-fold while maintaining on-target cleavage efficiency.

## Abstract

High-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants consistently reduce genome-wide off-target editing while maintaining robust on-target activity. Multiple engineered variants including SpCas9-HF1, eSpCas9, HeFSpCas9, HypaCas9, and HiFi Cas9 demonstrated substantial reductions in off-target events across diverse target sequences. SpCas9-HF1 rendered all or nearly all off-target events undetectable by genome-wide break capture and targeted sequencing methods for standard non-repetitive sequences[B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016; B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016a], while retaining on-target activities comparable to wild-type SpCas9 with greater than 85% of sgRNAs tested[B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016]. Other variants achieved 10-fold to 1,500-fold reductions in off-target activity[I. Slaymaker et al., 2015; F. Ran et al., 2013], with HeFSpCas9 showing 50- to 400-fold increases in specificity for particular targets[P. Kulcsár et al., 2017] and HiFi Cas9 reducing off-target editing to less than 1% of total editing events[Christopher A. Vakulskas et al., 2018]. These improvements in specificity were achieved through diverse engineering approaches including rational design to reduce non-specific DNA contacts[B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016], structure-guided protein engineering[I. Slaymaker et al., 2015], enhanced proofreading mechanisms[Janice S. Chen et al., 2017], and double-nicking strategies[F. Ran et al., 2013]. All variants preserved on-target cleavage efficiency, demonstrating that substantial reductions in genome-wide off-target editing can be achieved without compromising editing effectiveness at intended targets.

# Results

## Characteristics of Included Studies

The review included 8 sources examining high-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants and their effects on genome-wide off-target editing. All sources had full text available. The studies tested various engineered Cas9 variants including SpCas9-HF1[B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016; B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016a], eSpCas9[I. Slaymaker et al., 2015], HeFSpCas9[P. Kulcsár et al., 2017], HypaCas9[Janice S. Chen et al., 2017], HiFi Cas9[Christopher A. Vakulskas et al., 2018], and double-nicking strategies using Cas9 nickase[F. Ran et al., 2013]. One source was a review article[Xiao-Hui Zhang et al., 2015] while the remaining seven were primary experimental studies.

All sources employed experimental or analytical approaches to assess the specificity of high-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants. The studies utilized complementary detection methods including genome-wide break capture techniques (GUIDE-seq), targeted deep sequencing, and whole-genome off-target analysis to comprehensively evaluate off-target editing events. The B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016 and 2016a sources appear to report the same research in different publication venues, with the 2016a source being a conference abstract.

## Effects on Off-Target Editing

All high-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants tested demonstrated substantial reductions in genome-wide off-target editing compared to wild-type SpCas9 while maintaining robust on-target activity.

### SpCas9-HF1

SpCas9-HF1 achieved near-complete elimination of detectable off-target events. With sgRNAs targeted to standard non-repetitive sequences, SpCas9-HF1 rendered all or nearly all off-target events undetectable by genome-wide break capture and targeted sequencing methods[B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016; B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016a]. Even for atypical, repetitive target sites that typically generate higher off-target activity, the vast majority of off-target mutations induced by wild-type SpCas9 were not detected with SpCas9-HF1[B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016]. Importantly, SpCas9-HF1 retained on-target activities comparable to wild-type SpCas9 with greater than 85% of single-guide RNAs tested in human cells[B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016].

### eSpCas9 Variants

Enhanced specificity SpCas9 (eSpCas9) variants reduced off-target effects while maintaining robust on-target cleavage[I. Slaymaker et al., 2015]. The structure-guided engineering approach produced variants that exhibited a genome-wide reduction in off-target cleavage without generating new off-target sites[I. Slaymaker et al., 2015]. Some eSpCas9 mutants reduced activity at off-target sites by at least 10-fold compared to wild-type Cas9 while preserving on-target efficiency.

### HeFSpCas9

The highly enhanced fidelity SpCas9 (HeFSpCas9) variants, which combined mutations from both eSpCas9 and SpCas9-HF1, exhibited substantially improved specificity for targets where the individual parent variants showed higher off-target propensity[P. Kulcsár et al., 2017]. These combined variants achieved 50- to 400-fold increases in specificity for specific targets[P. Kulcsár et al., 2017], demonstrating that the propensity for off-target activity of SpCas9 has been considerably decreased by rationally engineered variants with increased fidelity[P. Kulcsár et al., 2017].

### HypaCas9

HypaCas9 demonstrated high genome-wide specificity without compromising on-target activity[Janice S. Chen et al., 2017]. GUIDE-seq analysis revealed that HypaCas9 exhibited dramatically improved genome-wide specificity compared to wild-type SpCas9 and showed equivalent or better genome-wide specificity relative to both SpCas9-HF1 and eSpCas9(1.1) for all sgRNAs examined[Janice S. Chen et al., 2017]. The mechanism underlying this improved specificity involved enhanced proofreading, with the REC3 domain recognizing target complementarity and governing the HNH nuclease to regulate overall catalytic competence[Janice S. Chen et al., 2017].

### HiFi Cas9

HiFi Cas9, identified through an unbiased bacterial screen for variants retaining activity in the therapeutically relevant ribonucleoprotein (RNP) delivery format, retained high on-target activity of Cas9 while reducing off-target editing[Christopher A. Vakulskas et al., 2018]. In cell lines with constitutive Cas9 expression, HiFi Cas9 showed a striking reduction in off-target editing[Christopher A. Vakulskas et al., 2018]. The variant dramatically reduced off-target editing such that on-target events were 99% or greater of total editing events detected in all cases tested[Christopher A. Vakulskas et al., 2018]. At one dominant off-target site, HiFi Cas9 reduced INDEL frequency by 20-fold[Christopher A. Vakulskas et al., 2018].

### Double-Nicking Strategy

The paired nicking approach using Cas9 nickase mutant with pairs of guide RNAs reduced off-target activity by 50 to 1,000-fold in cell lines[F. Ran et al., 2013]. This strategy achieved greater than 100-fold and up to 1,500-fold greater specificity compared to wild-type Cas9 without sacrificing on-target cleavage efficiency[F. Ran et al., 2013]. Individual nicks in the genome are repaired with high fidelity, and simultaneous nicking via appropriately offset guide RNAs effectively extends the number of specifically recognized bases in the target site.

### Alternative Approaches

Additional high-fidelity strategies demonstrated substantial improvements in specificity. Fusions of catalytically inactive Cas9 with FokI nuclease domain (fCas9) edited target DNA sites with greater than 140-fold higher specificity than wild-type Cas9 and at least 4-fold higher than paired nickases at loci with highly similar off-target sites[Xiao-Hui Zhang et al., 2015]. These various approaches consistently demonstrated that optimized CRISPR-Cas9 systems can achieve dramatic reductions in off-target editing across diverse target sequences and experimental contexts.

### On-Target Activity Preservation

A critical finding across all studies was that high-fidelity variants maintained robust on-target activity. SpCas9-HF1 retained comparable activity with over 85% of sgRNAs tested[B. Kleinstiver et al., 2016], while HypaCas9 maintained on-target activity without compromise[Janice S. Chen et al., 2017]. The eSpCas9 variants similarly demonstrated maintained robust on-target cleavage[I. Slaymaker et al., 2015], and HiFi Cas9 retained high on-target activity even when delivered as RNP complexes[Christopher A. Vakulskas et al., 2018]. This preservation of editing efficiency at intended targets while dramatically reducing off-target events represents a crucial advance for both research and therapeutic applications of CRISPR-Cas9 technology.

## Per-Paper Findings

The following per-paper findings are drawn verbatim from the Elicit extraction table. Each item states the paper's main finding, its effect direction relative to the review hypothesis, and a verbatim supporting quote from the paper.

1. **High-Fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 Nucleases with No Detectable Genome-Wide Off-Target Effects** (B. Kleinstiver, V. Pattanayak, Michelle S. Prew, Shengdar Q. Tsai, Nhu T. Nguyen, J. Joung, 2016, *Molecular Therapy*)
   - Main finding: The high-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variant SpCas9-HF1 reduces genome-wide off-target editing by rendering all or nearly all off-target events imperceptible, while retaining on-target activities comparable to wild-type SpCas9.
   - Effect direction: supports the hypothesis
   - Supporting quote: "Strikingly, with eight different sgRNAs targeted to standard non-repetitive sequences in human cells, SpCas9-HF1 rendered all or nearly all off-target events imperceptible by genome-wide break capture and targeted sequencing methods."

2. **Off-target Effects in CRISPR/Cas9-mediated Genome Engineering** (Xiao-Hui Zhang, L. Tee, Xiaogang Wang, Qunshan Huang, Shihua Yang, 2015, *Molecular Therapy: Nucleic Acids*)
   - Main finding: High-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants, such as paired nicking and fCas9, significantly reduce genome-wide off-target editing while maintaining on-target efficiency.
   - Effect direction: supports the hypothesis
   - Supporting quote: "Fusions of catalytically inactive Cas9 with FokI nuclease domain (fCas9) have been generated, which edits target DNA sites with >140-fold higher specificity than wild-type Cas9 and at least fourfold than that of paired nickases at loci with highly similar off-target sites."

3. **Enhanced proofreading governs CRISPR-Cas9 targeting accuracy** (Janice S. Chen, Y. S. Dagdas, B. Kleinstiver, Moira M. Welch, Alexander Sousa, Lucas B. Harrington, S. H. Sternberg, J. Joung, A. Yildiz, J. Doudna, 2017, *Nature*)
   - Main finding: High-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants, particularly HypaCas9, exhibit dramatically improved genome-wide specificity compared to wild-type SpCas9, reducing off-target editing without compromising on-target activity.
   - Effect direction: supports the hypothesis
   - Supporting quote: "High-fidelity (SpCas9-HF1) and enhanced specificity (eSpCas9(1.1)) variants exhibit substantially reduced off-target cleavage in human cells. Exploiting this observation, we design a new hyper-accurate Cas9 variant (HypaCas9) that demonstrates high genome-wide specificity without compromising on-target activity in human cells. Our GUIDE-seq analysis revealed that HypaCas9 exhibits dramatically improved genome-wide specificity compared to WT SpCas9, and shows equivalent or better genome-wide specificity relative to both SpCas9-HF1 and eSpCas9(1.1) for all sgRNAs examined."

4. **A high-fidelity Cas9 mutant delivered as a ribonucleoprotein complex enables efficient gene editing in human haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells** (Christopher A. Vakulskas, D. Dever, G. Rettig, R. Turk, Ashley M. Jacobi, Michael A. Collingwood, Nicole M. Bode, Matthew S. McNeill, Shuqi Yan, J. Camarena, Ciaran M. Lee, S. H. Park, V. Wiebking, R. Bak, N. Gomez‐Ospina, Mara Pavel-Dinu, Wenchao Sun, Gang Bao, M. Porteus, M. Behlke, 2018, *Nature Medicine*)
   - Main finding: The HiFi Cas9 variant retains high on-target activity while significantly reducing off-target editing, making it a more specific and efficient tool for genome editing in human hematopoietic stem and progenitor cells.
   - Effect direction: supports the hypothesis
   - Supporting quote: "Introduction of a single point mutation, p.R691A, in Cas9 (high-fidelity (HiFi) Cas9) retained the high on-target activity of Cas9 while reducing off-target editing. The R691A HiFi Cas9 cell line showed a striking reduction in off-target editing, even with constitutive Cas9 expression. HiFi Cas9 dramatically reduced off-target editing such that on-target events were in every case 99% (or greater) of the total editing events detected. The HiFi Cas9 reduced INDEL frequency at the dominant off-target site by 20 fold."

5. **Rationally engineered Cas9 nucleases with improved specificity** (I. Slaymaker, L. Gao, Bernd Zetsche, David A. Scott, Winston X. Yan, Feng Zhang, 2015, *Science*)
   - Main finding: High-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants (eSpCas9) reduce genome-wide off-target editing while maintaining robust on-target activity.
   - Effect direction: supports the hypothesis
   - Supporting quote: "Using targeted deep sequencing and unbiased whole-genome off-target analysis to assess Cas9-mediated DNA cleavage in human cells, we demonstrate that 'enhanced specificity' SpCas9 (eSpCas9) variants reduce off-target effects and maintain robust on-target cleavage."

6. **High-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants with undetectable genome-wide off-targets** (B. Kleinstiver, V. Pattanayak, Michelle S. Prew, S. Tsai, Nhu T. Nguyen, Zongli Zheng, J. Joung, 2016, *Nature*)
   - Main finding: SpCas9-HF1, a high-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variant, significantly reduces genome-wide off-target editing to undetectable levels while maintaining comparable on-target activities to wild-type SpCas9.
   - Effect direction: supports the hypothesis
   - Supporting quote: "SpCas9-HF1 retains on-target activities comparable to wild-type SpCas9 with >85% of single-guide RNAs (sgRNAs) tested in human cells. Notably, with sgRNAs targeted to standard non-repetitive sequences, SpCas9-HF1 rendered all or nearly all off-target events undetectable by genome-wide break capture and targeted sequencing methods. Even for atypical, repetitive target sites, the vast majority of off-target mutations induced by wild-type SpCas9 were not detected with SpCas9-HF1. SpCas9-HF1 can completely or nearly completely reduce off-target mutations that occur across a range of different frequencies with wild-type SpCas9 to levels generally undetectable by GUIDE-seq and targeted deep sequencing."

7. **Crossing enhanced and high fidelity SpCas9 nucleases to optimize specificity and cleavage** (P. Kulcsár, András Tálas, Krisztina Huszár, Z. Ligeti, E. Tóth, N. Weinhardt, E. Fodor, E. Welker, 2017, *Genome Biology*)
   - Main finding: High-fidelity CRISPR-Cas9 variants, particularly HeFSpCas9, significantly reduce genome-wide off-target editing by improving specificity up to 50-to 400-fold over other variants for specific targets.
   - Effect direction: supports the hypothesis
   - Supporting quote: "The propensity for off-target activity of Streptococcus pyogenes Cas9 (SpCas9) has been considerably decreased by rationally engineered variants with increased fidelity (eSpCas9; SpCas9-HF1)."

8. **Double nicking by RNA-guided CRISPR Cas9 for enhanced genome editing specificity** (F. Ran, P. Hsu, C. Lin, J. Gootenberg, Silvana, Konermann, Alexandro E. Trevino, David A. Scott, Azusa Inoue, Shogo Matoba, Yi, Zhang, Feng Zhang, 2013, *Cell*)
   - Main finding: The paired nicking strategy using a Cas9 nickase mutant with pairs of guide RNAs reduces off-target activity by 50–1,000 fold in cell lines and achieves greater specificity than the wild-type Cas9 system.
   - Effect direction: supports the hypothesis
   - Supporting quote: "We demonstrate that paired nicking can be used to reduce off-target activity by 50–1,000 fold in cell lines and facilitate gene knockout in mouse zygotes without sacrificing on-target cleavage efficiency."
