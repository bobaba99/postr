Postr · market & pricing strategy

The market is big enough. Reaching it is the whole problem.

Sizing the social-science & biology poster niche, testing the $20–30k/yr target against it, and settling sub-only vs. adding a cheap pack with real substitution data. The short version: the market is millions; a solo founder's organic reach, not market size, is what decides whether the target is hit.

The funnel — where 53,000 users/year sits

Target fields: psychology, sociology, public health, neuroscience, biology, medicine, nursing, education, ecology — the fields stuck with PowerPoint and no LaTeX escape. Excludes CS/eng/physics. SOLID counts from NSF NCSES, Eurostat, HESA; funnel narrowing is INFERENCE.

Is $20–30k/yr realistic?  Realistic but ambitious — and it's a distribution problem, not a market one.

$25k needs ~1,400 sales/yr → ~53,000 users/yr at 2.6% conversion. That's ~1.5% of the market but 7–13% of the serviceable market — the top of what a solo, unfunded, word-of-mouth tool realistically reaches in 3–4 years (comparable: Mind the Graph hit ~500k, BioRender 4M — but both took years and BioRender took venture money).

If conversion runs 4–5% (plausible — PowerPoint pain in these fields is acute), the same $25k needs only ~30k users and the target turns conservative. If word-of-mouth stalls at low-thousands (the common solo outcome), $3–8k is likelier. The lever is reach, not TAM.

The decision: sub-only, or add the pack?

The two variables that decide it

Everything else nets out. These don't.

Substitution = of pack buyers, the share who'd otherwise have bought the $18.99 term. Incremental = net-new buyers the cheaper pack unlocks (would've paid $0) per 100 would-be subscribers.

Why the pack probably recruits, not cannibalizes

  • SOLID Hybrid buyers are 7% of buyers but 25% of revenue (RevenueCat, 115k apps) — one-time buyers are a distinct high-value segment, not lost subscribers.
  • SOLID Education has the worst annual renewal (24%) but best weekly — your segment rejects long commitments and pays in bursts.
  • SOLID 88% of students stay free, and cap ~$5/mo — a subscription leaves the price-sensitive majority uncaptured; a $9.99 one-time reaches them.
  • Base case: ~35% substitution, ~65% incremental. The pack mostly serves people you'd otherwise earn nothing from.

If you add the pack, design it to recruit

  • Keep a real value gap: $9.99 / 3 decks vs $18.99 / 4-months-unlimited. Anyone making >~4 posters is better off with the term → heavy users self-select up (anchor effect, Ariely).
  • Don't show it side-by-side as a co-equal tier. Surface it as the lighter option, or at the moment someone declines the term (RevenueCat).
  • Price it at $9.99, not $4.99 — $4.99 keeps 82% after fees, $9.99 keeps 88%, and the curve flattens above that. $9.99 is the efficient point.
  • Don't undercut your own recurring revenue — if the pack is too generous, it just shifts would-be term money earlier.

Confidence. Market counts are sourced (NSF NCSES, Eurostat, HESA); the funnel narrowing to target fields and the SAM/SOM bands are inference — the softest number is "switch-willing share." The substitution rate (20/35/55%) is triangulated, not measured — no study directly tests pack-vs-sub substitution for a tool like this; validate it with your own first cohort. Pack-buyer repeat-purchase behaviour is unknown and assumed lower than term repurchase. LTV over ~3 years (6 poster seasons), net of Lemon Squeezy fees + LLM cost. The single biggest real-world unknown is organic distribution reach — every comparable that scaled had years and/or funding.
