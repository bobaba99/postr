Postr · deck pricing & the abuse question

Abuse is a rounding error. The trial is the whole game.

Four ways to price deck generation, projected over 3 years with Lemon Squeezy fees and trial abuse both baked in. The worry was multi-account abuse of a free trial. The math says something you can act on: a stolen deck costs you 5.6 cents, so even rampant abuse barely moves the outcome — while whether a free deck makes people buy swings it by tens of thousands.

The finding, before you touch a slider

Drag Abuse rate from 0% to 100% — the winning balance barely flinches (a stolen deck is $0.056). Drag Trial uplift and it moves ~100× more. So the abuse question you asked has a clean answer: log it, don't fight it. The real decision is whether letting people see one free deck converts them — and if it does even a little, the free trial wins going away.

Why abuse can't hurt you here

  • SOLID Stripe (Mar 2026): 7.4% of AI-startup signups show multi-account abuse — but abuse tracks payoff per account.
  • SOLID GitLab got farmed only because free CI minutes had crypto-mining resale value. A 2-cent deck has none.
  • Postr's payoff per fake account is ~100× below the products reporting 7.4%. Expect casual manual duplication, not bot farms.
  • MATH 15% abuse × $0.056 = under a cent of waste per real free deck. Immaterial next to real deck COGS.

The proportionate defense (near-zero conversion cost)

  • Normalize email — strip Gmail dots & +tags, lowercase, before the uniqueness check.
  • Block disposable domains (maintained list).
  • Email-verify before the free deck unlocks; gate the one-shot server-side.
  • Hash the email on delete → detect delete-and-recreate. This is the record you wanted; salted one-way hash, documented as fraud-prevention.
  • AVOID phone/SMS or card — they cost 5–37% of conversions to stop 2 cents. GitLab: one card validates many accounts anyway.
