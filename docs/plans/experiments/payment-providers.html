Who should collect the $19.

Six providers, judged for Postr's actual case: a $19 one-time term and a $4.99 pack, sold to researchers worldwide, by a solo Canadian founder on a Supabase stack. At this size the real question isn't the fee — it's who is the legal seller, and therefore who owns the worldwide tax mess.

✓ Recommended
Lemon Squeezy
Full Merchant of Record — your worldwide VAT/sales-tax liability disappears. Native one-time payments, no seller-approval gauntlet, and it's Stripe-owned (2024), so low platform risk. The most mature webhook→Supabase path for flipping your plan column. 5% + $0.50.
Runner-up
Polar
Same MoR benefit, most developer-native API. Cheaper on paid tiers as you scale. Trade-off: youngest, pricing recently in flux.
✕ Not for launch
Plain Stripe
Cheapest fees, but not an MoR — leaves you liable for EU VAT & US sales tax everywhere. That's the whole problem you're avoiding. Also skip: Shopify (wrong tool), Gumroad (~15–23% at your prices), Paddle (approval friction, sub-$10 custom pricing).

The one axis that decides it: Merchant of Record vs. gateway

When a Munich grad student buys your $19 term, EU rules charge German VAT (19%) from the first euro — there's no small-seller threshold for a non-EU seller. With a plain gateway (Stripe) you register for EU VAT OSS, file quarterly, track US state nexus, and carry the liability. With a Merchant of Record the provider is the legal seller — it charges, files, and remits that VAT; your tax life becomes "declare the payouts as Canadian income." The ~2–3 pt fee premium is trivial against avoiding even one EU VAT registration (~€2–5k). For this business, choose an MoR.

Why the $4.99 pack is the fee trap

The rest of the decision

Integration (Supabase + webhook)

All MoRs use the same shape: hosted checkout → provider POSTs a webhook → your Express endpoint verifies the HMAC signature → Supabase service-role update of the plan column. Keep that key server-side only.

  • Stripe / Lemon Squeezy — most examples, best-documented Supabase guides.
  • Polar — cleanest API, fewer battle-tested Supabase examples yet.
  • Gumroad — simplest but storefront-redirect, least "in-app".

Chargebacks & disputes

MoRs absorb the fraud/dispute burden as the merchant — real de-risking for a solo founder. Polar charges $15/dispute; Lemon Squeezy folds most into the blended fee.

Plain Stripe: you handle disputes and pay ~CA$15 per chargeback (refunded only if you win). Academic buyers are low-fraud, so this is a modest factor — but "not your problem" still has value.
